Mobile checkout loses 47% of shoppers. Desktop loses 19%.

A funnel analysis of 60,000 simulated store visitors, built with SQL and Python. Pick a channel or device to see where that group drops off.

What the data shows

Mobile is the leak. It brings 62% of visitors but converts at 5.7%, against 9.1% on desktop. The gap sits between starting checkout and entering payment details.

Channels differ a lot. Email converts best and social worst. Paid search is a quarter of all traffic, and 39% of its visitors leave before viewing a product.

A late-September bug. For two weeks only 51 to 59% of mobile shoppers who reached payment completed the order, against about 86% normally. That cost roughly 130 orders.

How it was built

Raw events go into SQLite. One SQL view turns them into a strict, ordered funnel: unique users, each step after the one before it, all within 7 days of the first visit. Duplicate events are removed by taking each user’s first occurrence of every step. Python then draws the charts and this page’s data.

MIN(CASE WHEN event_name = 'product_view' THEN event_ts END) AS t2,
...
CASE WHEN r2 = 1 AND t3 IS NOT NULL AND t3 >= t2
     AND t3 <= datetime(t1, '+7 days') THEN 1 ELSE 0 END AS r3

The write-up ends with three recommendations: audit the mobile checkout, alert on payment completion by device, and rebalance paid-search spend. Read the full write-up on GitHub.
